I didn’t start this because I thought I could fix anything.

I started because doing nothing felt worse.


People keep asking what difference it makes.

Like change is supposed to be visible right away.

Like it should feel immediate.


But nothing on this scale works like that.

The planet doesn’t move at our pace.

It never has.


Ice melts slowly.

Oceans rise quietly.

Forests disappear one section at a time.


By the time you can feel it, it’s already been happening for years.


We are living inside changes we can’t fully perceive.


I think that’s why people struggle to care.

Not because they don’t want to.

Because nothing feels urgent until it’s too late.


Everything we react to is immediate.

Messages. Notifications. Deadlines.

Things that demand attention now.


But the things that matter most don’t work like that.

They don’t demand attention.

They just continue.


Slowly.



I tried explaining this once.

No one really knew what I meant.

I don’t think I did either.


How do you explain something you can’t actually see?

How do you prove something that hasn’t fully happened yet?


That’s the problem.


The Anthropocene means we are part of something larger than ourselves.

Not just living in it.

Changing it.


Every small action contributes to something that takes years to understand.

Years to measure.

Years to even notice.


That doesn’t make it small.


It makes it invisible.



I know I won’t see the outcome of anything I do.

I know I won’t be there when it matters most.


That’s the part no one talks about.

Doing something you’ll never see the result of.


Planting something you won’t watch grow.


Acting anyway.



Maybe nothing I do matters on its own.

Maybe it’s too small to measure.


But nothing only happens on its own.


Everything builds.

Everything accumulates.

Everything leaves something behind.


Even if no one notices it right away.



I think about time differently now.

Not in days or weeks.

But in what continues after I’m gone.


That’s the scale this works on.


Not now.

Later.

Much later.



And I know that’s hard to care about.

I know it doesn’t feel real.

I know it doesn’t feel urgent.


But that doesn’t make it unimportant.


It just means we aren’t built to understand it.



Still.

I think it matters.

So I’ll keep going.




Crutzen (2002); Chakrabarty (2009); Clark (2011); Wajcman (2015)
